# Dogfooding `vas`, 2026-10-23 — the Me38 AMENDMENT 4 rental (seven runs chained on one RTX 5090, after one unreachable box; askesis)

One box for the whole job: askesis's Me38 AMENDMENT 4, seven AR training runs chained on one RTX 5090 (two on a
new, larger corpus, then five more seeds of a known recipe), ~12.5 h, launched 10:17 CEST. Two boxes were
rented to get it: the first never answered `ssh`. `vas` 0.2.0, read-only, from the launching PC.

## What worked

- **`OTHER` answered "is anyone else on the GPU?" in one cell.** At ~10:56 (the box's age then, 1 h 13 min, from its start at 09:42:44 by the API), `vas --ssh --wide` read the box at
  98 %, 7.9 of 32 GB, and `-` for memory held by anyone else: our training was alone on the card. On 10-18 the
  same question took `nvidia-smi`, `hmn ps`, `lsof`, `fuser` and a support ticket.
- **The `POWER` column explained a slowdown before anyone went looking for one.** It read
  `500/575W+power_cap`, with `CLOCK` 2272/3090 MHz under load. The training's own speed check had measured this
  box ~15 % slower, 40 minutes earlier, than the 10-22 box (see the measurement below); the cap was the first
  explanation on the screen, not a suspicion about our code.
- **`--wide` filled a gap in the rentals log.** It gave machine 12031 and host 73118 at once; the row for the
  10-22 box still reads "machine and host IDs: Éric's dashboard, to add".
- **`--log` with a glob follows a chain by itself.** `--log "/root/src/askesis/reference/canvas/data/runs/*/run.log"`
  picks the newest file, i.e. the run in progress, whichever of the seven it is:
  `me38_c12_s0_d1/run.log: epoch 19 | train 0.0118 | val(ema) 0.0042`.
- **The money line:** `credit $25.67 | burn $0.693/h over 1 running | ~37.1 h left at this rate`, with
  `COST~ 0.85` on the box at 1 h 13 min: the job's budget was visible without opening the dashboard.
- **A new login banner did not leak into the table.** This box's `ssh` greeting now carries an extra line
  addressed to AI agents (pointing to a guide on the instance). Our own scripts had to filter it out of their
  output; `vas`'s table was clean.

## A measurement: what a 500 W cap costs (one pair of boxes, confounded)

The 10-18 report left this open ("Not measured: how much slower the training runs for it"). The same speed
check (`me38_speedcheck.sh`: per-step time from 20- and 200-step runs, so fixed costs cancel) ran on two
RTX 5090 boxes four days apart:

| per step | AR, full width | AR, trimmed | O (bidirectional) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10-22 box: power limit 600 W, EPYC 7642 | 79.9 ms | 46.7 ms | 86.7 ms |
| 10-23 box: power limit 500 W (`power_cap` active), EPYC 7B12 | 89.8 ms | 53.7 ms | 98.6 ms |
| ratio | 1.12 | 1.15 | 1.14 |

So, for this workload (fp32, a 10.8M-parameter transformer, batch 128), a 500 W cap costs about 12-15 % per
step, and it was NOT priced in: the capped box cost more ($0.693/h) than the 600 W one ($0.652/h), so the
price per step was ~22 % higher (trimmed, the setting the runs use; ~20 % at full width). The offer listing does not show the cap, so a renter cannot weigh it. NOT a clean
measurement: the hosts' CPUs, drivers (590.48 against 615.78) and loads differ too. The trimmed/full ratio
(0.58 against 0.60) suggests the host's CPU did not bind on either box. The 10-18 boxes, at 400 W (1695 MHz),
were not timed this way.

1. **"Running but unreachable", as a reading of its own.** The first box (instance 57337748, France,

   - What `vas` already does: `--ssh` prints `ssh`'s own error in the row (FAQ: "A box shows an error instead
     of GPU numbers"). I ran plain `vas` at ~09:20, not `vas --ssh`, so the error was not on the screen: the
     first lesson is for the user.
   - What it could add: the age beside the error, as one label (`UNREACHABLE 4m: connection refused`),
     because the age is what the destroy-or-wait decision rests on, and three rentals now say the same thing.
   - **The direct route.** `vas` shows the proxy only. When the proxy refused, Éric had to fetch the direct
     address from the dashboard. Showing it (`--wide`: the instance's public IP and its mapped port 22) and
     probing it when the proxy refuses would turn the second check into a column. To confirm first: which
     fields of the instance row carry it.
2. **Waiting for a line in a log.** To catch the first training loss, printed once at the end of a 20-minute
   stage, I wrote a bash loop: `ssh` every 2 minutes, `grep` for `initial loss`, stop after two `ssh` failures,
   give up after an hour. `--log` cannot do it: it prints the last non-blank line, and the line I needed is
   followed within seconds by the stage's evaluation lines, so it is never the last line when a look comes. Two options would replace such loops:
   - `--grep PATTERN` (with `--log`): the last line MATCHING, instead of the last line;
   - `--until PATTERN` (with `--watch`): exit 0 when the pattern appears, so a script can wait on it. The
     stop-after-failures rule would live in `vas`, where every such watcher gets it, instead of in each
     hand-written loop.
3. **Run `vas --wide` on every box rented, even one destroyed at once.** The French box's machine id was never
   read, so it cannot go into `--exclude-machine`, and the next search may offer it again. A workflow note more
   than a feature; a `--credit-log`-like history of the boxes seen (instance, machine, host, first and last
   status) would keep the ids without asking anyone to remember.

7. **"Is this the box we had?" took the raw API (added 2026-10-24).** The Swedish offer looked like the 10-22
   box, which ran five runs cleanly at full power. `vas offers` shows host and price, not what identifies a

8. **DLPerf predicted the power cap before any `ssh` (added 2026-10-24; one pair, to confirm).** Two US boxes,
   both RTX 5090, both listed at 108.1 TFLOPS and ~1,456 GB/s: Texas (DLPerf 198.9, $0.536/h) and Indiana
   (165.6, $0.673/h). `vas --ssh --wide` (16:43:49) then read `POWER` 575W on Texas and `475/600W` on Indiana.
   The DLPerf ratio (0.83) matches the power ratio (475/575 = 0.83). Indiana was destroyed unused; Texas runs
   the job. The Swedish machine showed DLPerf 197-199 on the dashboard, with 575 W listed as its maximum power.
   - If more pairs agree, `vas offers` could show DLPerf and DLPerf per dollar, and mark an offer whose DLPerf
     is well below its GPU model's median in the same search as "likely capped": the 10-23 measurement says a
     cap costs 12-15 % per step and is not priced in.
   - To measure: tonight's speed check on the Texas box gives its per-step time, to set beside its DLPerf and
     the 10-22 and 10-23 boxes'.
   - **A counterexample (2026-10-25).** Texas's speed check matched the fastest box measured (O at 85.8 ms per
     step, DLPerf 198.9, 575 W). But two South Korean RTX 5090s (host 406325, machines 145061 and 142476) both
     report DLPerf **199.2** and are both capped at **500/600 W** (`vas --ssh`, 10:16 and 10:19). DLPerf did not
     see this cap: it may be measured before a host sets its limit, or a 500 W cap may not move vast.ai's
     benchmark. So DLPerf alone cannot mark "likely capped"; only the reading on the card (`POWER`) can, and
     note 8's proposal should be dropped or kept only as a weak hint beside it.
9. **Unverified is not deverified: a filter that tells them apart (added 2026-10-24).** vast.ai's documentation
   (docs.vast.ai, "verification stages") defines three statuses. Verified: "passed automated checks for
   reliability, network stability, operational health, and performance", continuously monitored. Unverified:
   "Newly added machines or machines under evaluation", "not a judgment of quality". Deverified: "A previously
   Verified machine no longer meets requirements" ("sustained degradation": network, hardware or system errors,
   abuse, or the host's own workloads). Verification checks no speed and no power cap.
   - Today's evidence. Unverified Texas (machine 147897, a high id, as a new machine would have) runs at full
     power, and its speed check matched the fastest box we have measured (O at 85.8 ms per step). Verified
     Indiana was capped at 475 W. The deverified Swedish machine never started three instances in 2-17 minutes.
     So, for a rental: unverified is acceptable when our own checks pass (`vas --ssh`'s power, the preflight,
     the speed check); deverified is a reason to skip.
   - `--verified` cannot express that: it hides both. `vas offers` already shows the difference in its
     `VERIFICATION` column. A `--no-deverified` flag (hide deverified offers, keep unverified ones) would encode
     the rule, and could become askesis's config default in place of `--verified`.
   - To confirm first: how the search API marks a machine that is new against one that was deverified (both
     "appear as Unverified" on the website, per the same page, while the API's `verification` field said
     `deverified` and `unverified` today).
